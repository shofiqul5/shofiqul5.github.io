05 · THEORY

Who Controls Your Emotions?

Marcus Aurelius, the Roman emperor and Stoic philosopher, once said something that is still uncomfortable to read: “Curse me, disgrace me, or praise me, and none of it matters to me. If it does matter, then believe it: I am your slave.”

Think about what this means. When someone praises you, you feel proud and happy. When someone insults you, you feel hurt or angry. It seems natural, but Marcus Aurelius asks you to look at it differently. If another person’s words can make you happy or miserable at will, that person holds the controls of your emotions. They can raise your mood or ruin it with a single sentence.

That is why he says that if a person standing in front of him can stir any emotion in him, he is not free. He is that person’s slave. Real freedom is not only about chains or walls. It is also about whether your inner peace depends on other people’s opinions.

This does not mean you should become cold or stop feeling anything. The point is to choose your response instead of reacting automatically. Praise and insults are just words, and what they mean to you is your decision. When you stop handing that decision to others, nobody can control you with their tongue.

The next time someone’s words make you angry or overjoyed, ask yourself: “Am I the one in control right now, or are they?”
